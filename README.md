# Hi :technologist:

![Header](./img/header.png)

I'm a programmer who's always looking for solutions to complex problems. I once tried to refactor Hegel's dialectic into three clean functions: `thesis()`, `antithesis()` and `synthesis()`. The synthesis keeps returning a new thesis, so technically I shipped an infinite loop.
 
It started when I was 11, answering strangers' questions on forums with far more confidence than knowledge. Twenty years later the ratio has improved a little. More than 14 of those were spent leading and building backend systems in banking, e-commerce and consulting, mostly with **Java**, **Kotlin** and **AWS**, and I learned that the hardest bugs are rarely in the code.
 
These days I'm building **[Randan](https://endoffile.com.br)**, a platform that helps motorcycle delivery cooperatives stand up to the big delivery apps. It's the kind of problem I like: real people, tight margins, and no room for an architecture that only looks good on a slide.
 
Outside of work I read philosophy and mathematics, which is a polite way of saying I enjoy problems that don't compile. I contributed to Gecko back in the day, taught Assembly for a while, and still believe that understanding how things work underneath is never wasted time.
 
But don't worry, I'm fine. I just need a strong coffee and an old song to function.
